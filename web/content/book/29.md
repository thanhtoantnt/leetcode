Linear programming: maximize/minimize a linear objective subject to linear inequalities. The chapter formalizes LP (standard and slack forms), proves the simplex method correct, and covers duality.

## The shape of an LP

Variables ≥ 0, a linear objective, constraint inequalities. Geometrically: the feasible region is a polyhedron (intersection of half-spaces), and the optimum sits at a vertex. The whole game is walking vertices.

## The simplex algorithm

Convert to slack form (inequalities become equations with slack variables); a **basic solution** sets nonbasic variables to 0. Simplex pivots: bring one nonbasic variable in, kick one basic variable out — geometrically, slide along an edge to an adjacent vertex, choosing an entering variable that improves the objective. Terminate when no improving direction exists (or detect unboundedness). Degenerate pivots (no movement) can cycle; Bland's rule breaks ties to prevent it. Exponential worst cases exist (Klee-Minty), but in practice simplex is fast — and the ellipsoid/Karmarkar interior-point methods that followed are polynomial.

## Duality

Every primal max problem has a dual min problem: constraint coefficients transpose into the objective, RHS values become constraint bounds. **Weak duality** (any feasible primal value ≤ any feasible dual value) is a two-line proof; **strong duality** (simplex's final slack form yields an optimal dual solution, values equal) means the bound is tight. The certificate reading: an optimal dual gives per-constraint prices proving you can't do better. Max-flow/min-cut (Ch. 26) is duality in graph clothing; minimax game arguments are too.

## Why it's in an algorithms course

LP is the mother problem: many NP-hard problems are LP with integrality constraints dropped (LP relaxation, the gateway to approximation algorithms — Ch. 35). When you can phrase an optimization as linear constraints, decades of machinery apply; when the natural variables must be integers, you're in IP-land and hardness arrives fast.
