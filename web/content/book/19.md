Fibonacci heaps chase a table of amortized bounds where binary heaps can't follow: INSERT, MINIMUM, and UNION in **O(1)** amortized — the union being the party trick. The price: worst-case per-op can be bad, and constants are large. Chapter 17's potential method is the analysis engine.

## The design idea: procrastination

A Fibonacci heap is a root list of heap-ordered trees plus a min pointer. The operations that can be cheap *are made cheap immediately*; everything else is deferred:

- **INSERT** — splice one node into the root list, maybe update min. O(1).
- **UNION** — concatenate two root lists, take the better min. O(1), no structure touching at all.
- **EXTRACT-MIN** — the garbage-collection point: remove the min, promote its children to the root list, then **consolidate** — repeatedly link roots of equal degree until all root degrees are distinct (the binomial-heap merge dance, using an A[degree] array). Amortized O(lg n).
- **DECREASE-KEY** — cut the node from its parent, promote to root list. If the parent had already lost a child, cut it too, cascading upward (marked nodes track "already lost one child"). Amortized O(1).

The potential function Φ = (#roots) + 2·(#marked nodes) makes the accounting work: fast operations grow Φ (they add roots), slow operations burn it. Cutting children from parents is what *breaks* binomial structure — hence "Fibonacci": with cascading cuts, a node of degree k must have subtree size ≥ F(k+2) (Fibonacci), so degree ≤ log_φ n ≈ 1.44 lg n. The golden ratio bounds the consolidation array.

## Why it matters (and why you'll never write one)

The payoff is asymptotic: Dijkstra and Prim drop from O(E lg V) to O(E + V lg V) — the E edges leave the logarithm. In practice the constants (pointer surgery, marked bits, root-list churn) lose to a plain binary heap for realistic sizes; real Dijkstra implementations use pairing heaps or binary heaps. Historical note: Fredman–Tarjan invented it to prove a bound, and it held the shortest-paths record for decades.

The transferable idea is the engineering pattern: **defer maintenance to a batch point**. Lazy insertion + periodic consolidation is the same trade as Chapter 17's dynamic tables, Chapter 6's BUILD-HEAP being O(n) vs n inserts at O(lg n), and Python's `list` amortization. When single operations must be O(1) but a global cleanup is acceptable, procrastinate.
