Multithreaded algorithms: the one chapter about parallelism. The model is "dynamic multithreading" — fork/spawn and sync keywords plus a scheduler — the abstraction behind Cilk and, loosely, Go's goroutines and JavaScript's async.

## The model

A multithreaded computation is a DAG of strands (instructions between forks) with spawn edges (fork a subtask, don't wait) and continuation edges. Two metrics replace running time:

- **work** T₁ — total instructions, one processor
- **span** T_∞ — longest path in the DAG, infinitely many processors

The laws: T_P ≥ max(T₁/P, T_∞) — speedup is capped by both available parallelism and the critical path. Greedy scheduling achieves T_P ≤ T₁/P + T_∞ (near-optimal when work dominates span: parallel slack).

The practical skill is **reducing span**: parallelize the recursion structure, not the loops. Parallel Fibonacci exposes both calls; parallel matrix multiply splits quadrants (span drops from cubic to ~n^lg 7-ish structure); multithreaded merge sort parallelizes the merge with a span-reducing split — the span of plain merge sort is Θ(n) (the sequential merge), and fixing it is a genuinely clever exercise.

Why it matters here: LeetCode never asks for it, but the vocabulary (work vs span, critical path) is the right mental model for understanding why some pipelines can't be sped up by adding workers — the dependency chain is the floor.
