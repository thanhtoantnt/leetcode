String matching: find pattern P (length m) in text T (length n) in O(n) time instead of the naive O(nm). The `strings/` and `sliding_windows/` folders are the interview-sized versions of exactly this chapter.

## 32.1 Naive

Try every shift, compare m characters: Θ((n−m+1)·m) worst case (`aaaa...` vs `aaab`). No preprocessing, no memory of what previous mismatches taught you.

## 32.2 Rabin-Karp — rolling hash

Treat the window as a base-d number mod a prime q. Slide the window: subtract the leading digit, add the trailing one — O(1) per shift (the rolling hash, which is also every sliding-window-hash solution in `sliding_windows/`). On hash match, verify characters (spurious hits). Expected O(n+m) with a good q and few matches; worst case still Θ(nm). Bonus: matches *all* occurrences naturally, and the same fingerprint checks whether two files/strings are equal (Exercise 32.2-4 — the checksum trick).

## 32.3 Finite automata

Preprocess P into a DFA: state = length of the longest prefix of P that is a suffix of the text consumed so far. Then scan T once, one transition per character — O(n) matching after Θ(m³·|Σ|)-ish preprocessing (improvable). The idea that matters: the next comparison depends only on *how much of the pattern currently matches*, not on where you are in the text.

## 32.4 Knuth-Morris-Pratt

The automaton compressed to its essence. The **prefix function** π[q] = length of the longest proper prefix of P[1..q] that is also its suffix. On mismatch after q characters, don't back up the text pointer — jump the pattern to π[q] and continue. Preprocessing is O(m) (the prefix function computes itself recursively), matching O(n), linear guaranteed, no hashing randomness.

The mindset — "on failure, fall back to the longest border (prefix=suffix) and keep going" — powers KMP, the Z-algorithm, and half the `strings/` hard problems (repeated substring patterns, shortest palindrome by appending).
