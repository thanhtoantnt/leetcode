Maximum flow: push as much as possible from source s to sink t through capacity-limited edges. The chapter builds the Ford-Fulkerson *method* (a framework, not one algorithm), proves the max-flow/min-cut theorem, and tours the descendants — bipartite matching plus the push-relabel family.

## 26.1 Flow networks

A flow assigns each edge a value ≤ its capacity, with conservation (inflow = outflow at every internal vertex). |f| = net flow out of s. Antiparallel edges are normalized away with a dummy vertex; multiple sources/sinks merge into supersource/sink — modeling hygiene worth stealing for interview setups.

## 26.2 Ford-Fulkerson and min-cut

The **residual network** G_f: for each edge, capacity c−f forward (room to add) and f backward (room to *undo*). An **augmenting path** is any s→t path in G_f. The method: repeatedly find an augmenting path, push its bottleneck (min residual capacity), rebuild residuals. Termination: no augmenting path.

The undo edge is the subtle part — later augmentations can cancel earlier flow, rerouting through the reverse direction. That's why "greedily fill shortest/fattest paths" isn't obviously correct, and why the proof needs:

**Max-flow min-cut:** a cut (S,T) has capacity = sum of S→T edge capacities. Net flow across any cut equals |f| — so |f| ≤ c(S,T) for every cut. When Ford-Fulkerson stops, the reachable-in-residual set defines a cut whose capacity equals |f|: flow = min cut. Duality in the flesh: the bottleneck *proves* optimality.

Integer capacities → integer max flow (each augmentation adds ≥ 1), but the number of augmentations depends on capacities — O(E·|f*|), not polynomial in input size for irrational capacities. BFS-chosen augmenting paths (Edmonds-Karp) makes it O(VE²) and is the implementation to write.

## 26.3 Bipartite matching

Model matches as flow: source→left vertices (cap 1), left→right edges (cap 1), right→sink (cap 1). Max flow = max matching. The classic reduction showing flow's reach — many "assignment with constraints" problems are matchings in disguise.

## 26.4–26.5 Push-relabel

The modern family: instead of paths, push excess flow *locally* (a vertex with more inflow than outflow pushes to a lower-labeled neighbor) and *relabel* (raise a vertex's label when stuck). Pre-flow allows temporary conservation violation, discharged later. Relabel-to-front: O(V³). These underpin the fastest practical max-flow codes; the local-repair viewpoint also powers competitive-programming Dinics-style implementations.
