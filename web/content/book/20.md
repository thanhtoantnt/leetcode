van Emde Boas trees answer predecessor-style queries on integers in O(lg lg u) — doubly logarithmic in the *universe size* u, not in n. The trick: recursively halve the universe with the bits of the key itself, and keep min/max cached so recursion happens on only one side.

## The road there

A bitvector of u slots answers member/min/max in O(u) worst case; O(1) if you could scan words at once. Split it: u/√u clusters of √u bits each, plus a *summary* bitvector marking which clusters are nonempty. Now `high(x)` = x's cluster, `low(x)` = position within it. MEMBER recurses once: Θ(lg u) for one-sided queries.

The problem: SUCCESSOR may recurse *twice* — once in the cluster, once in the summary — giving Θ(lg u · lg lg u). Kill the second recursion by the chapter's two moves:

1. **Store min (and max) explicitly per node** — the minimum is *not* recursed into clusters; it sits in the object. Checking `x < V.min` answers "successor is min" with zero recursion. min plays the role the summary would have needed.
2. After consulting min, successor needs only one side: either the answer is inside x's cluster, or the summary says which next cluster — and that call takes the *cached min* of the target cluster, not another successor search.

Every operation recurses on a √u universe exactly once: T(u) = T(√u) + O(1) → **O(lg lg u)**. Space O(u) — the cost of the speed (hashing the clusters shrinks it in practice).

## What it buys and costs

For integer keys with known universe u, vEB beats heaps (O(1) member/min) and BSTs (O(lg lg u) successor) — the classic "integers are more powerful than comparisons" theme from Chapter 8. The price: O(u) space, and u must be fixed up front. In practice, y-fast trees (O(n) space, same bounds) or 4-ary heap/trie hybrids show up instead; for LeetCode, this chapter is an idea more than a tool.

The transferable bits:

- **recursive bit-splitting** — high/low decomposition is exactly how radix sort (Ch. 8) and the `bit_manipulate/` tricks think
- **caching the extremal answer to break a two-branch recursion** — the min-in-node move is the same instinct as Chapter 14's subtree aggregates: store what you'd otherwise recompute, and make it O(1) to read
