Approximation algorithms: when the problem is NP-hard (Ch. 34) but you still need an answer with a guarantee. An ρ-approximation returns a solution within a factor ρ of optimal, in polynomial time.

## 35.1 Vertex cover — factor 2

Repeatedly pick any uncovered edge, take *both* endpoints, delete covered edges. The chosen edges are a matching (disjoint), and any cover must contain ≥1 vertex per matching edge — so OPT ≥ #edges-picked, while the algorithm takes 2 per edge. Ratio 2, greedy, one pass. A lower bound on OPT from a relaxed structure (the matching) is the whole proof technique: **compare your output to a bound, not to the unknown optimum**.

## 35.2 TSP — factor 2 with triangle inequality; unapproximable without

Compute an MST, walk its preorder traversal (shortcutting revisits). MST ≤ OPT (remove an edge from the optimal tour → a spanning tree), and the doubled-tree walk costs ≤ 2·MST ≤ 2·OPT. Triangle inequality is what makes shortcutting safe.

Without it, TSP cannot be approximated within *any* constant factor unless P = NP (the Ch. 34 reduction with 0/1 costs makes ratios encode Hamiltonicity). The lesson generalizes: approximation guarantees are properties of the problem's structure, and the triangle inequality earns its keep.

## 35.3 Set cover — ln n

Greedy: pick the set covering the most uncovered elements. A weighting argument gives H(n) ≈ ln n approximation — and ln n is essentially tight unless P = NP. Greedy with a coverage/counting potential is the standard heuristic for hard covering problems, with a proof-shaped reason it's not terrible.

## 35.4 Randomization + LP

LP relaxation (Ch. 29) plus randomized rounding: solve the fractional version, round each variable with probability equal to its fractional value. A bridge between the LP world and discrete solutions — the professional's version of "solve the relaxation, then fix it up."

## 35.5 Subset sum — an FPTAS

Dynamic programming on target sums is pseudo-polynomial (O(n·t) — explodes with the *value* of t, which is why subset-sum is NP-hard yet `dynamic/0416` works at small scale). Trimming the state list to 1+ε-spaced values gives (1+ε) approximation in time polynomial in n and 1/ε — a **fully polynomial-time approximation scheme**. The technique: coarsen the DP state space; pay accuracy for speed, smoothly.
